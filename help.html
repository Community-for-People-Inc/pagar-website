FAQ

Getting started
How do I create an account?▾

Download the app, enter your mobile number, and verify the one-time PIN sent by SMS. Add your name and email to finish.

How do I book a ride or order?▾

Open the service you need, set pickup/drop-off or pick a store, and confirm. Fares and totals are shown upfront before you commit.

Payments
What payment methods can I use?▾

Cash on delivery, GCash, Maya, and major e-wallets. For rides you may also pay cash directly to the driver.

Do you honor PWD, Senior and Student discounts?▾

Yes, once your ID is verified. It is not automatic: upload your PWD, Senior Citizen or Student ID in the app, and the discount applies after it is verified, where the law requires. See the PWD / Senior / Student Discounts policy below.

Cancellations & safety
Can I cancel a food order?▾

Food is free to cancel before the restaurant accepts it. Once the kitchen starts preparing, it may no longer be cancellable. Full rules in Cancellation & Refunds below.

How does Pagar keep me safe?▾

Driver name, photo, plate and rating before you ride; in-app chat so you never have to give out your number; live trip sharing; and SOS to a real team in Mindanao. If you do call your driver, you will each see the other's real number. See the Safety page.

For partners
How do I become a driver or seller?▾

Head to the Partners page and apply as a Hero (driver) or merchant. Our Marawi team helps you set up — free at launch.

Part B · Customers

B1. Fares and what they are made of

Fares are calculated by the platform and shown before you confirm. A ride fare is built from a base amount and distance, per the applicable fare matrix. On top of the fare, the following may apply and are shown to you:

  • a service fee — what CFPI charges for the platform (A1);
  • a pick-up fee where the driver travels to reach you (this is the driver's, in full);
  • waiting time, as set out below;
  • tolls, parking or terminal fees actually incurred.

Waiting time today. On a ride, waiting runs from the driver's arrival and the first 5 minutes are free; after that it is ₱2 a minute, capped. On a delivery, errand or parcel there is no waiting charge at all right now. That is what the apps charge, and it is what you will be billed.

This is going to change, and you will be told before it does. It has been decided that waiting will be ₱15 for each 15 minutes in whole blocks, after 15 free minutes at a stop, at the same rate for every service — and, for a prepaid order left at the door, after the first 10 minutes as B7a describes. It is not built yet. Until it is, the rates in the paragraph above are what apply. When it is built, a waiting charge will be billed to the account that booked the job: for a parcel, that is the sender, not whoever is at the door.

Errands. The fee for a pabili errand is the road distance between the shop you pin and the drop-off you pin, fixed when you book. Choosing a different shop later does not re-price it.

PAGAR does not charge you more for choosing one way to pay over another. The service fee is worked out from the fare, not from your payment method, and it is the same whether you pay in cash or in the app.

Tricycle fares follow the Marawi City fare ordinance and are never surged. For vehicle types not covered by the ordinance, fares may rise with demand; when they do, the higher price is shown before you confirm, never added afterwards.

B2. Changing the trip

If you change the destination or add stops, the fare is recalculated. A driver may decline a change that is unsafe or outside their service area.

B3. Payment

During the pilot you pay in cash. Cash on delivery is the only payment method in the app while the pilot runs. The PAGAR wallet top-up, Maya and card are not open yet, and checkout says so. Cash is offered only for drop-offs inside Marawi City. When online payment opens it will be processed by Maya, and the methods offered at checkout will be shown in the app. This clause will be updated before any other method opens. A Teen Account (B12) requests rides only, and its parent or guardian is responsible for what it books. Once an electronic payment method is open, if a payment fails the amount stays owing and we may ask you to settle it before booking again.

Cash limits. Cash on delivery for a food, marketplace or pabili order is capped at ₱500 per order. Rides and package deliveries take cash at any amount. For food, marketplace and pabili orders, the number of completed bookings needed before cash is offered is set for each city. In Marawi City during the pilot it is zero, so a first order may be paid in cash. Other cities start at 3 completed bookings. Any completed booking counts towards it — a ride, a food order, a marketplace order or a package — and the app shows how many you have and how many you still need. Rides and package deliveries take cash from your first booking, in every city.

You must pay for a service you received. Deliberately avoiding payment may result in suspension.

B7. Cancellations and no-shows

You can cancel in the app, up to the point each service allows. Cancellation rules are shown at the time and may change. Repeated cancellation or failing to appear may lead to limits on your account.

A ride cannot be cancelled once the driver has reached your pick-up point. Until the driver arrives you can cancel in the app as usual. From the moment they arrive, neither you nor the driver can cancel — if you are not there, the driver's way out is to report a no-show (B7a), which is a different thing and has its own rules. Once you are on board, a ride is not cancelled by anyone. It ends at the drop-off. If a trip is left unfinished because the driver stops responding with you on board, support closes it at the fare you were quoted — it is not left open against you, and you are not charged for the time it sat.

Cancellation fee — parcels, pabili and marketplace orders. Cancelling is free until a rider has accepted your order. Once a rider has accepted it, a cancellation costs half (50%) of the order's delivery or errand fee — the rider's fare plus PAGAR's fee on it, never the price of the goods. The same fee applies when your order is cancelled because you did not reply to the rider's two calls and two chat messages before pickup (B7a). Such an order is cancelled rather than handed to another rider, and nobody is paid for it. The app tells you the amount before you cancel. Nothing is taken at the time: the fee is added to your next parcel, pabili or marketplace order, shown there as its own line, and paid with that order — in cash at the door on a cash order. It is shared between the rider who accepted the cancelled order and PAGAR, in the same proportions as a fare, and the rider's share is paid when the fee is collected. Food orders carry no cancellation fee. Food follows GrabFood's rule instead.

Food cannot be cancelled once the kitchen is preparing it. Until then you can cancel it in the app. After that, food already cooking is not refunded (B8).

B7b. Confirming a cash food order before the kitchen starts

When you place a food order paying cash on delivery, we hold it for 15 minutes and ask you to confirm it in the app. The restaurant is not told about your order and does not start cooking until you do. If you do not confirm within 15 minutes, the order is cancelled automatically.

Nothing is charged and nothing is owed when that happens. You have paid nothing, no food was made, and no rider was sent — a cancellation here costs you nothing at all, and it is not counted against your account under B7 or B7a.

Why we ask. A cash order that fails after the food is cooked costs the rider a wasted trip and the kitchen a wasted meal, and B7a describes what follows for you. This step exists so that far fewer orders ever reach that point: fifteen minutes of your time, before anything is made, instead of a failed delivery afterwards. Order when you are able to answer.

B7a. If you are not there for your order

This clause exists because a cash order that fails costs three people: the rider has made the trip, the kitchen has cooked the food, and you have nothing.

An order you paid for in the app is delivered, never sent back. A food or marketplace order you paid for in advance is not refused or refunded at the door. If you do not answer when the rider arrives, they call you twice and send you two chat messages in the app, the second at least 3 minutes after the first, and they wait at the door for 10 minutes from when they arrived. If you have not replied by then — 10 minutes after they arrived, and 5 minutes after their first try — they leave the order at the address, somewhere out of sight of the street, and photograph where they left it. It then counts as delivered, and nothing is refunded. Waiting past those 10 minutes is charged under B1, to the account that booked the order. A reply in the chat restarts the 5 minutes. If something is wrong with the order itself — the wrong food, or damaged goods — tell us through in-app support (B8).

What we ask of you. If you choose cash on delivery, be at the address you gave, be reachable on the mobile number on your account, and accept and pay for the order when it arrives. If your plans change, cancel in the app rather than letting the rider travel.

What the rider does. They will try to reach you by chat or phone before and on arrival, and wait a short time at the address. We record when they arrived and that they tried to make contact, so that a rider who followed the process is not treated as being at fault. On a marketplace cash order the app will not let them collect your goods until they have messaged or called you — so if you are not reachable, you will usually hear from them before anyone has set off.

If you do not reply before pickup. If the rider cannot reach you before collecting your order — or, for a ride, at the pickup — they call you twice and send you two chat messages in the app, the second at least 3 minutes after the first. If you have not replied 5 minutes after their first try, they may hand the order back to the other riders, close a pabili before anything is bought, or end the ride as a no-show. On a cash food order the rider may hand it back to the pool once. If the next rider cannot reach you either, the order is cancelled, with no fee, and you must order again. On a cash order, cash on delivery is then turned off for your account, for every service including rides, just as if the order had been refused at the door. Every missed reply is recorded on your account, cash or prepaid, and we tell you each time. Nothing is charged to you for it. A reply in the chat restarts the 5 minutes. A parcel whose recipient does not reply is not counted against the sender.

If a cash marketplace order cannot be handed over, the goods go back to the seller. PAGAR charges you nothing — not the goods and not the trip. But the rider made the trip both ways on your order: paying them for it is between you and the rider, and PAGAR does not pay it for you. A cash food order cannot be returned — it has been cooked — so it simply ends as undeliverable. A pabili that you do not accept at the door is closed, and PAGAR charges you nothing for it. But the rider bought the shopping on your instructions, with their own money: you must pay them for it at the receipt price. That is between you and the rider — PAGAR does not pay it for you, and does not collect it. An errand fee you paid in advance is not refunded.

What we may do if the order cannot be handed over. We may treat it as a cancellation by you, and we may:

  • remove cash on delivery from your account. This one happens automatically the first time a cash order cannot be handed over, or you do not reply before pickup on a cash order (above), and it covers every service, rides included. What that leaves you depends on how the app can be paid at the time. Where it offers a way to pay in advance, future orders must be paid in advance — you can still order, you simply cannot choose cash. While cash on delivery is the only way to pay in the app, you cannot place a new order until cash is turned back on; contact us through in-app support. Nothing is billed to you for the order that failed. If you think it was applied wrongly, ask us to review it;
  • suspend your account, or close it permanently where a booking was never genuine. This is not automatic — a person decides it.

We will tell you which of these we have applied and why. Where cash has been removed, the app says so at checkout rather than simply hiding the option. You can dispute any of it through in-app support, and an administrator can give cash back.

Merchants and riders. Where an order fails through no fault of the merchant, we may compensate them for food already prepared. That decision is ours and is made case by case on the record of the delivery — it is not automatic, and this clause is not a promise of payment to a merchant.

Prevention, so this happens less. Cash on delivery for a food, marketplace or pabili order is capped at ₱500 per order. Above that, the order cannot be paid in cash. Rides and package deliveries take cash at any amount. In Marawi City during the pilot, food, marketplace and pabili orders can be paid in cash from the first order. Elsewhere they need 3 completed bookings first (B3). Rides and package deliveries take cash from your first booking.

B8. Refunds

Refunds of money PAGAR took from you in the app are credited to your PAGAR wallet (B4), not returned to the original payment method.

We refund money PAGAR actually took from you in the app, on a job that did not finish. Three things therefore cannot be refunded:

  • A job that finished. An order delivered to you, or a ride that reached the drop-off, is not refunded — neither in whole nor line by line. This includes an item you report missing from an order that was delivered, and a safety report about a ride that reached its destination. Report both anyway — we look into it, and it can lead to action against the driver or the merchant under A11 and D11. What follows a completed job is not money back for it.
  • An order nothing was paid on. If no money was taken, there is nothing to give back.
  • Food the kitchen has started cooking. Once preparation begins, the order cannot be cancelled for a refund (B7).
  • A prepaid food order that could not be delivered. Food is perishable, and the money has paid for a meal that was made. Nothing is refunded for it.
  • A cash ride or a cash parcel. You paid the rider directly, in cash, and PAGAR never held that money — so PAGAR cannot return it. What PAGAR took is its own commission, and it took that from the rider's wallet, not from you.

These are checked twice: when a refund is asked for, and again before any money moves.

B9. Motorcycle (habal-habal) rides

Habal-habal rides are not offered yet (A2). This section is the rules that apply once they open, and is kept so the terms do not change at the moment the service does.

A habal-habal seats the passenger directly against the driver, so these rides are matched same-gender only. You will be asked your gender when booking one; if it cannot be established you will be offered a tricycle or a car.

Helmets must be worn, and the driver provides one. One passenger per motorcycle — no second rider, and no cargo bulky enough to unbalance the bike; a driver may refuse a load on that basis and is expected to.

You may be asked to show ID before a habal-habal ride ("no ID, no entry"). This exists to make the same-gender rule mean something: it is only as good as our confidence that the person at the kerb is the person who booked. A driver may decline the trip if identity cannot be established.

In line with the Children's Safety on Motorcycles Act (RA 10666), a child who does not meet the legal requirements may not ride as a motorcycle-taxi passenger, and a minor who may ride must be accompanied by a parent or guardian.

B10. Identity selfie

Because the same-gender rule is only as reliable as our confidence that the account belongs to the person who shows up, we may ask you to take a selfie before your first ride. It is asked separately, at that moment, with its own consent — and only for rides, never for food, package or marketplace orders.

We never ask a teen for a selfie, a photo, or an ID. Identity assurance on PAGAR runs toward the people who provide the service — drivers are verified, and re-verified while working (C6) — because that is what protects a passenger. For a teen the pickup PIN (B12) already answers the question a photo would, at the kerb, at the moment it matters, without us holding a child's face image afterwards.

B11. Audio recording on trips

For everyone's safety, PAGAR can record audio inside the vehicle during a trip.

  • We ask you separately at sign-up, and you can change your answer any time in your settings. Accepting these Terms is not by itself your agreement to be recorded.
  • The driver agrees separately too, in their own app. Under Republic Act 4200 a recording is lawful only where every person present has agreed.
  • Nothing is recorded without your agreement — recording runs only while everyone involved has it switched on.
  • If you do not agree, the trip goes ahead without recording.
  • The recording stays private on the phone and is not sent to us. There is no central store of trip audio to breach, because we do not hold one. It deletes itself after 7 days.
  • We cannot listen to it unless it is sent to us with a safety report.
  • For a teen's trip, nobody at PAGAR can listen unless the parent or guardian specifically agrees to that recording being retrieved. A safety report on its own is not enough. If they cannot be reached, it is not retrievable — that is deliberate.
  • Once retrieved it is kept up to 30 days and heard only by authorised staff, who must record who they are and why.
  • The driver can see that recording is running. Their screen shows it for as long as it does. RA 4200 is about recordings made secretly, so a standing agreement is not the whole of it — the other party has to be able to watch it happen.

If you start a recording yourself. The Safety Centre lets you record a trip from your own phone. It is the same rule and not an exception to it: it will not start unless your driver has already agreed to being recorded, and their screen shows it running. The file stays on your phone, we cannot hear it, and it deletes itself after 7 days unless you attach it to a safety report. This works on any trip. If your driver withdraws their agreement during the trip, your recording stops; what was recorded until then stays on your phone.

Anyone else riding with you has to agree too. Before the recording starts, the app asks whether anyone else is in the vehicle and, if so, asks you to confirm that they have all agreed. We keep your answer, with the time and the trip. If someone riding with you has not agreed, do not record.

On a teen's trip, both recording switches count. A recording the teen starts themselves runs only while the teen's own switch and the parent or guardian's switch are both on, and stops if either is switched off during the trip.

Apart from a recording you start yourself, recording applies to teen, PWD and senior trips, and only where everyone involved has switched it on: the driver, and the passenger. For a teen, both the teen and the parent or guardian each have their own switch, and recording needs both. Each person agrees once and can switch it off at any time in their profile. If anyone has it off, the trip goes ahead without recording.

B14a. If nobody takes your parcel

If you chose to have it left, the courier leaves it at the address and photographs where they left it. It then counts as delivered, and the photo is on your receipt. Choose this only where it is safe to leave a parcel. A recipient who was there and refused it still sends it back.

Otherwise it comes back to you. The courier does not leave a parcel at an address with nobody to receive it unless you asked for that. They bring it back to the pickup address, and you, the sender, pay for the trip back:

If the courier did not try to reach the recipient, or did not wait, the trip back is not charged to you. A recipient who does not reply is not counted against you as the sender (B7a).

If the sender cannot be reached. If nobody at the drop-off takes the parcel and you have given no instruction, the courier calls you. If you do not answer either, they may try the recipient once more, with the wait starting again. If that fails, the courier may hand the parcel in at PAGAR's office in Marawi City. D8. Store hours and pausing

Set your opening hours and pause orders when you are busy. If you set no hours, your store is treated as always open — so set them.

Hours you publish are a promise to the customer, and we expect you to honour them. Somebody ordering at 8pm because your store says it is open until 9pm has relied on that. If you cannot serve, pause the store rather than letting orders arrive and fail — a paused store costs you an order; a store that accepts and then cannot deliver costs a customer their evening and a rider their trip. Describe your items accurately, including ingredients that commonly cause allergic reactions. Where you present a product as certified halal, you are responsible for holding and maintaining that certification. D10. Orders, cancellations and substitutions

Accept or decline promptly. Do not substitute items without the customer's agreement. Repeated failure to respond, cancellation after acceptance, or unagreed substitution may lead to suspension.

Have the order ready before the rider arrives. On food orders a rider is dispatched while you are still cooking, so by the time they reach you they have already made the trip and their next job is waiting. The preparation time you set is what we promise the customer and what we send the rider on — set it honestly, and raise it when you are busy rather than letting every order run late. A rider kept waiting is the one cost in this arrangement that falls on somebody who had no part in it.

Once you start preparing a food order it can no longer be cancelled, and food already cooking is not refunded (B8).

D10a. When a customer says something was missing or wrong

This is the part of these Terms most likely to cost you money, so it is set out in full.

A refund can still arise on an order that did not finish, and that is the case this clause is about: an order nobody delivered, cancelled after you had begun, or one we could not complete.
