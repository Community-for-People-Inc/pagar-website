FAQ

Questions, answered.

Getting started
How do I create an account?▾

Download the app, enter your mobile number, and verify the one-time PIN sent by SMS. Add your name and email to finish.

How do I book a ride or order?▾

Open the service you need, set pickup/drop-off or pick a store, and confirm. Fares and totals are shown upfront before you commit.

Payments
What payment methods can I use?▾

Cash on delivery, GCash, Maya, and major e-wallets. For rides you may also pay cash directly to the driver.

Do you honor PWD, Senior and Student discounts?▾

Yes. Verify your ID once in the app and eligible discounts apply automatically where the law requires. See the discounts policy below.

Cancellations & safety
Can I cancel a food order?▾

Food is free to cancel before the restaurant accepts it. Once the kitchen starts preparing, it may no longer be cancellable. Full rules in Cancellation & Refunds below.

How does Pagar keep me safe?▾

Driver name, photo, plate and rating before you ride; in-app chat so you never have to give out your number; live trip sharing; and SOS to a real team in Mindanao. If you do call your driver, you will each see the other's real number — we do not mask them. See the Safety page.

For partners
How do I become a driver or seller?▾

Head to the Partners page and apply as a Hero (driver) or merchant. Our Marawi team helps you set up — free at launch.

B7a. If you are not there for a cash order

This clause exists because a cash order that fails costs three people: the rider has made the trip, the kitchen has cooked the food, and you have nothing.

What we ask of you. If you choose cash on delivery, be at the address you gave, be reachable on the mobile number on your account, and accept and pay for the order when it arrives. If your plans change, cancel in the app rather than letting the rider travel.

What the rider does. They will try to reach you by chat or phone before and on arrival, and wait a short time at the address. We record when they arrived and that they tried to make contact, so that a rider who followed the process is not treated as being at fault. On a marketplace cash order the app will not let them collect your goods until they have messaged or called you — so if you are not reachable, you will usually hear from them before anyone has set off.

If a marketplace order cannot be handed over, the goods go back to the seller — and the trip is still owed. The rider is paid for both legs, out and back: they made the journey twice, and it was not their doing that nobody took the order. That round trip is charged to you — both legs of the delivery fee — and nothing else is: you are not billed for the goods, which went back to the seller. Paying it clears the matter, including the cash restriction above. A food order cannot be returned — it has been cooked — so it simply ends as undeliverable.

What we may do if the order cannot be handed over. We may treat it as a cancellation by you, and we may:

  • remove cash on delivery from your account, so future orders must be paid in advance. This one happens automatically the first time a cash order cannot be handed over — you can still order, you simply cannot choose cash. Settling what you owe for that trip restores it: once you have paid, the matter is closed and you are not penalised on top. If you owe nothing and it is still in place, ask us to review it;
  • suspend your account, or close it permanently where a booking was never genuine. This is not automatic — a person decides it.

We will tell you which of these we have applied and why. Where cash has been removed, the app says so at checkout rather than simply hiding the option. You can dispute any of it through in-app support, and an administrator can give cash back.

Merchants and riders. Where an order fails through no fault of the merchant, we may compensate them for food already prepared. That decision is ours and is made case by case on the record of the delivery — it is not automatic, and this clause is not a promise of payment to a merchant.

Prevention, so this happens less. Cash on delivery is capped at ₱1,000 per order. Above that the app will not accept cash and asks you to pay online instead.

The other two remain RESERVED RIGHTS exercised by a human, and are still deliberately not described as machinery: there is no device or number matching to catch a repeat offender behind a new account, and no merchant claim or reimbursement flow — a merchant reports through ordinary in-app support today. Please check that the automatic removal of a payment method on a first incident, with notice at checkout and an appeal route, is proportionate as drafted. The ₱1,000 cap IS enforced server-side on food, marketplace and package.

B9. Motorcycle (habal-habal) rides

A habal-habal seats the passenger directly against the driver, so these rides are matched same-gender only. You will be asked your gender when booking one; if it cannot be established you will be offered a tricycle or a car.

Helmets must be worn. In line with the Children's Safety on Motorcycles Act (RA 10666), a child who does not meet the legal requirements may not ride as a motorcycle-taxi passenger.

B10. Identity selfie

Because the same-gender rule is only as reliable as our confidence that the account belongs to the person who shows up, we may ask you to take a selfie before your first ride. It is asked separately, at that moment, with its own consent — and only for rides, never for food, package or marketplace orders.

We never ask a teen for a selfie, a photo, or an ID. Identity assurance on PAGAR runs toward the people who provide the service — drivers are verified, and re-verified while working (C6) — because that is what protects a passenger. For a teen the pickup PIN (B12) already answers the question a photo would, at the kerb, at the moment it matters, without us holding a child's face image afterwards.

B11. Audio recording on trips

For everyone's safety, PAGAR can record audio inside the vehicle during a trip.

  • We ask you separately at sign-up, and you can change your answer any time in your settings. Accepting these Terms is not by itself your agreement to be recorded.
  • The driver agrees separately too, in their own app. Under Republic Act 4200 a recording is lawful only where every person present has agreed.
  • Nothing is recorded secretly — both apps show recording running, for the whole trip.
  • If you do not agree, we may provide the trip without recording, or decline to provide it.
  • The recording stays encrypted on the phone and is not sent to us. There is no central store of trip audio to breach, because we do not hold one. It deletes itself after 72 hours.
  • Nobody can simply play it back — not you, not your driver, not us.
  • For a teen's trip, nobody at PAGAR can listen unless the parent or guardian specifically agrees to that recording being retrieved. A safety report on its own is not enough. If they cannot be reached, it is not retrievable — that is deliberate.
  • Once retrieved it is kept up to 90 days and heard only by authorised staff, who must record who they are and why.

Recording currently applies to Teen Account trips.

B12. Teen Accounts (ages 13–17)

A parent or guardian links a Teen Account to their own, confirms the teen's age, agrees on their behalf, and sets what the teen may book — the hours, the vehicle types, and whether only same-gender drivers are matched. Motorcycle rides are off unless the guardian turns them on. The guardian can pause or unlink at any time, and the link ends automatically on the teen's 18th birthday.

When setting up the account the guardian gives the teen's name and birthday, and may optionally add their school. The birthday is what confirms they are 13–17 and what ends the account on their 18th birthday. School is optional, is only for the family's own reference, and is never shared with drivers — we ask for it because families set up trips to and from school, not because anything in the app reads it.

Two contacts. Alongside the guardian who set up the account, the teen names their own emergency contact — a second adult who must not be that same guardian. We ask how they are related to the teen, and they must be 18 or over. They are invited to PAGAR and must accept before they receive anything: an emergency contact is sent the teen's safety alerts, and nobody is enrolled into receiving a child's information without agreeing to it. Either the teen or the contact can end that arrangement at any time.

We require a second, different person because one contact is a single point of failure — a parent may be driving, asleep, or out of load — and because a young person should always have someone else they can reach.

On a teen trip: only drivers specifically approved to carry teen passengers are matched; the teen gets a 4-digit PIN the driver must enter before the trip starts; the guardian is notified at booking, acceptance, start and completion, can follow the trip live, and both contacts are alerted immediately on any emergency alert; and the fare is charged to the guardian unless they allowed the teen to pay cash.

PAGAR also monitors the trip while it is under way. If the vehicle stops unusually long, leaves the expected route, or we lose its location, we message the teen and tell the guardian at the same time.

If the trip is recorded (B11), the recording is also kept automatically when an emergency alert is raised, when we check on the trip and the teen does not reply, or when the guardian asks — because a young passenger cannot always be expected to report something themselves. Keeping a recording does not mean anyone has listened to it, and nobody can, unless the guardian separately agrees to that specific recording being retrieved.

B13. Food and marketplace orders

The restaurant or seller is the seller of record. They are responsible for the food or goods, their accuracy, safety and quality. Prices are set by them and shown VAT-inclusive. Where an item is unavailable, the merchant may propose a substitution, which you can accept or decline.

Report a problem with an order in-app; we will work with the merchant on a resolution, which may include wallet credit.

B14. Package delivery — what you may not send

Do not send: illegal drugs; weapons, ammunition or explosives; flammable, corrosive or hazardous materials; live animals; cash, cheques or bearer instruments; human remains; counterfeit goods; or anything barred by law. You are responsible for what you hand over, and for any loss or penalty caused by sending a prohibited item.

Someone else's contact details. A package booking asks you for a recipient's name, address and mobile number, and that person has usually never used PAGAR and has agreed to nothing. So when you enter their details you confirm that you are entitled to give them to us — that you have the person's permission, or another lawful basis, to share their name, address and number with PAGAR and with the courier who will deliver to them.

We use those details only to make the delivery: to route the courier, and so the courier can call or message the recipient to hand the parcel over. The recipient will see the courier's number and the courier will see theirs, on the same terms as A8a. If a recipient asks us what we hold about them, or asks us to delete it, they can write to privacy@communityforpeople.org and we will deal with them directly as the person the data is about — we may need to tell them who sent the parcel in order to explain why we hold their number at all.

B15. Package value and loss — no insurance is offered yet

PAGAR does not currently sell parcel insurance, and you are not charged for any. Insurance cover is something we intend to offer once it is arranged with a licensed provider. Until then it is not available, and this clause says so rather than implying cover that does not exist.

What this means for you now: send valuables at your own risk. Declare fragile or high-value items to the courier before handover, pack them properly, and consider whether the item should travel by PAGAR at all. Where an item is lost or damaged through CFPI's or a partner's fault, your rights under the Civil Code and the Consumer Act (RA 7394) are unaffected — A13 does not take those away.

When cover becomes available it will be offered as a separate, priced, opt-in product with its own terms, its named underwriter, its exclusions, and a written claims process. It will never be switched on silently.

B16. Lost items

Tell us as soon as you can — open the completed trip or order in the app and choose "I left something behind". It reaches our team with the job already attached, which matters: the sooner we call, the likelier the driver still has it and is still nearby.

We will try to contact the driver, but CFPI cannot guarantee recovery and is not responsible for items left behind.

Getting it back is a trip, and you may have to pay for it. If the item is found, returning it means the driver making a journey they were not booked for. We will tell you what that trip costs and ask you before it is made — you can decide to collect it yourself instead. Nothing is charged to you automatically.

B17. Accessibility

PAGAR is for everyone. Refusing a passenger because of a disability, an assistance animal, or a mobility aid is a breach of these Terms by the partner and is acted on.

Part C · Drivers

You are an independent contractor, not a CFPI employee. You choose when and whether to work, and you are free to work for other platforms.

C1. Getting approved

You must be 18 or older, hold a valid driver's licence and OR/CR for the vehicle, and pass our verification — including an NBI or police clearance, and a medical clearance where applicable. Documents are reviewed by our team and stored securely. Keep them current: an expired document suspends you until it is renewed. Approval can be withdrawn.

C2. What you earn, and what PAGAR takes
  • The fare is yours, per the applicable fare matrix.
  • PAGAR charges a platform commission on the fare, collected from your in-app wallet on completion — whether the passenger paid cash or electronically.
  • The pick-up fee for travelling to the passenger is 100% yours, on top of the fare.
  • Waiting-time pay, where it applies, is yours.
  • Tips are 100% yours. PAGAR takes no share of a tip.
  • Payouts go to the payout method on your profile on the published schedule.

Every completed job shows a breakdown of what you earned and what was deducted.

C3. Statutory discounts come out of the fare — read this one

When a passenger uses a PWD or Senior Citizen discount on a ride, the discount is deducted from the base fare before your share is calculated. You therefore bear the substantial majority of that discount, not the platform.

This is stated plainly because it directly affects what you are paid, and because it works the opposite way on food orders, where the restaurant bears it. Every discounted trip shows the discount in your earnings breakdown.

C4. Wallet balance and cash jobs

To take cash and cash-on-delivery work you must keep a positive wallet balance, because the commission on those jobs is collected from it. Cash you collect on a COD order is money you hold on CFPI's behalf and is settled against your wallet. There is a cap on how much collected cash you may carry before further jobs are withheld.

C5. Location tracking

While you are online, your location is tracked continuously — to match you with nearby jobs, to show passengers where you are, and for safety. It stops when you go offline.

C5a. The customer's number, and your own

When you accept a job you are given the customer's name and mobile number, and they are given yours — your real number, not a masked or platform number. Calls between you go phone-to-phone. On a package job you also get the recipient's name and number, and that person may never have used PAGAR at all.

Your own network's charges apply to a call you make. PAGAR does not pay for your calls and does not reimburse load. In-app chat costs you no load and no call charge — use it for the ordinary "I'm outside" message and save calling for when you genuinely cannot find someone.

The number is for that job and nothing else. Do not save a customer's or recipient's number to your own contacts, message or call them once the job is finished, add them on any other app, share or sell their number, or use it to solicit work outside PAGAR. Do not use it to contact them about a rating, a dispute or a cancellation — that goes through support. Any of this is a breach of A10 and of these Terms, and it is the kind of thing that ends a driver's access permanently rather than earning a warning. Customers can report it in the app, and we act on those reports.

C6. Identity check-ins

We may ask you to take a selfie at intervals while working, to confirm the person driving is the approved driver. This is what makes the same-gender rule meaningful rather than a promise. Skipping or failing one stops you taking new jobs until it is resolved; an administrator can clear it.

Never let another person use your account. It is the fastest way to be permanently removed.

C7. Same-gender matching, and prayer breaks

Motorcycle rides are matched same-gender only, based on your verified gender. You may pause for Salah or Jummah; you take no new jobs while paused and resume when ready. Pausing does not count against you.

C8. Teen passengers

Carrying teen passengers is not automatic. Drivers join the teen list in one of two ways:

  • On the recommendation of your barangay captain. This is how the list is built at the start, when nobody has a long record on PAGAR yet. The captain who recommended you is named on the record.
  • On your record with us, once you have a strong rating over a real number of completed trips.

Either way an administrator makes the entry and the reason is written down. Teen trips are audio-recorded end to end.

You must agree to trip recording separately, in your own app, before you can accept a teen trip. Under RA 4200 a recording needs the agreement of everyone present, and yours is one of them. You can withdraw at any time; doing so simply removes you from teen trips. On a teen trip the passenger gives you a 4-digit PIN, which you enter before starting.

The recording is not sent to PAGAR and stays on the passenger's phone, encrypted, for 72 hours before deleting itself. Nobody — including us — can play it back unless there is a safety report and the passenger's guardian specifically agrees to it being retrieved.

C9. Your obligations on the road

Follow traffic law. Carry the required helmets. Do not exceed passenger limits. Do not drive under the influence. Do not use your phone unsafely. Do not solicit payment outside the app or ask a passenger to cancel and pay in cash. Do not discriminate against any passenger.

C10. Insurance — not yet in place

PAGAR does not currently provide accident cover for drivers or passengers. We intend to arrange it with a licensed provider, and until it exists these Terms say so plainly rather than letting you assume cover you do not have.

You must carry whatever insurance the law requires for your vehicle — including CTPL — and keeping it valid is your responsibility. Some other platforms advertise personal-accident cover; PAGAR does not offer it yet, and you should not rely on it.

When cover is arranged, its scope, its underwriter and its exclusions will be published here and communicated to you before it takes effect.

C11. Tax

You are responsible for your own tax registration and filings on what you earn. CFPI provides earnings records and withholds or reports where the law requires.

C12. Deactivation and appeal

We may suspend or remove you for safety violations, fraud, document lapses, account sharing, or a pattern of complaints. We will tell you why, and you may appeal to support@communityforpeople.org. Amounts already earned remain payable.

Part D · Merchants (restaurants and marketplace sellers)

D1. You are the seller

You are an independent business and the seller of record. You are responsible for the accuracy of your listings, the safety and quality of your goods, and for holding the permits your business requires — including LGU business permits, and sanitary or FDA requirements where they apply to food.

D2. Getting approved

We verify your business before you can sell. Keep your documents current. Approval can be withdrawn.

D3. Commission and payouts

PAGAR charges a commission on the goods subtotal. Commission is charged on the discounted subtotal — you are not charged commission on money nobody paid. Payouts follow the published settlement schedule, with a statement showing each order and what was deducted.

D4. Statutory discounts come out of your goods revenue

When a customer uses a PWD or Senior Citizen discount on a food order, you bear it as the seller of record — not CFPI, and not the driver. The relief applies to the customer's own meal on the restaurant bill. This is the reverse of how ride discounts work (C3), and is stated plainly for the same reason.

The delivery fee is never discounted — it belongs to the driver.

D5. VAT and receipts

Menu prices you enter are VAT-inclusive. Where your business is VAT-registered, VAT is extracted from the price you set — never added on top — so the price the customer sees is the price you entered. Businesses below the statutory annual threshold are treated as non-VAT.

You remain responsible for your own tax registration, filings and the issuance of your own receipts.

D6. Promotions you run and fund

You may run your own promotions — happy hour, lunch deals, closing sales. These are funded by you, from your own goods revenue, never from a CFPI budget and never from the delivery fee. Your promotion appears on your own storefront; it reaches app-wide discovery only if you have bought a placement (D7).

D7. Advertising

Placements are sold at a flat fee and are clearly labelled. Buying a placement does not move your business up any list or ranking — there is no auction and no paid ranking. Some categories are not accepted, including alcohol, gambling, lending, political content, and anything conflicting with local norms.

D8. Store hours and pausing

Set your opening hours and pause orders when you are busy. If you set no hours, your store is treated as always open — so set them.

D9. Product information, allergens and halal

Describe your items accurately, including ingredients that commonly cause allergic reactions. Where you present a product as certified halal, you are responsible for holding and maintaining that certification. Everything on PAGAR is assumed halal by default in this market; the meaningful distinction is certified versus not, and nothing is labelled negatively.

D10. Orders, cancellations and substitutions

Accept or decline promptly. Do not substitute items without the customer's agreement. Repeated failure to respond, cancellation after acceptance, or unagreed substitution may lead to suspension.

An order shows you the customer's name and mobile number, and the assigned rider's number, so you can sort out a substitution, a missing item or a delivery that cannot be completed. It is their real number — nothing is masked. Use it for that order only: do not keep it after the order is done, add it to a mailing or broadcast list, message customers with promotions, or pass it to anyone else. Marketing to PAGAR customers is done through PAGAR (D7), never from a number you collected off an order. Misuse is grounds for suspension under D11 and breaches A10.

D11. Suspension and appeal

The same terms as C12 apply: we say why, and you may appeal.

PAGAR · operated by Community for People, Inc. · Effective: October 4, 2026

PAGAR is a delivery and transport platform operated by Community for People, Inc. ("CFPI"), a non-profit organization serving Marawi City and the Bangsamoro region. This policy explains what personal information we collect, how we use it, and your rights over it.

1. Information we collect
  • Account details: name, mobile number, email, and password.
  • Verification documents you choose to upload (e.g. a valid ID for discounts, or — for drivers, restaurants, and sellers — licenses, clearances, permits, and payout details).
  • Location data, to show nearby drivers, calculate fares and routes, and enable live trip tracking. Precise location is collected only while you are booking or an order/ride is active.
  • Transaction records: your orders, rides, packages, fares, and payment method used.
  • Device and usage data: device identifiers and app activity needed to operate and secure the service.
2. How we use your information

To provide the service — match you with drivers and merchants, process bookings and payments, calculate fares, and enable tracking; to keep the platform safe (identity verification, the same-gender motorcycle rule, and emergency/SOS response); to provide customer support; and to meet legal, tax, and regulatory obligations.

3. When we share information
  • With drivers and merchants, only what is needed to fulfil your order or ride (e.g. name, pickup/drop-off, contact for the trip).
  • With payment providers (such as Maya / GCash) to process transactions.
  • With mapping providers to power addresses and routing.
  • With regulators or authorities where required by law, or to protect safety.

We do not sell your personal information.

4. Retention and deletion

You can delete your account anytime from Profile → "Delete my account", or by emailing privacy@communityforpeople.org. Deletion removes your personal information and disables the account. Records of completed transactions are retained in anonymized form (no longer linked to you) where required for legal, tax, and accounting purposes.

5. Your rights (Data Privacy Act of 2012)

You have the right to be informed, to access and correct your data, to object to processing, and to have your data erased or blocked, subject to legal limits. You may also lodge a complaint with the National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph).

6. Security

We protect your information with access controls and encryption in transit, and limit access to authorized personnel. No system is perfectly secure, but we work to safeguard your data.

7. Children

Accounts are for users 18 and older. In line with the Children's Safety on Motorcycles Act (RA 10666), children below the required age may not ride as motorcycle-taxi passengers.

8. Changes & contact

We may update this policy and will note the effective date above. Questions or requests: privacy@communityforpeople.org.
